Lesson 16: Bees and Flowers
pollinate — to carry pollen from one flower to another so the plant can make seeds

nectar — the sweet liquid inside flowers that bees collect to make honey

hive — the structure where a colony of bees lives

transfer — to move something from one place to another

bloom — to open into a flower

Example

Bees and flowers need each other in ways that scientists call a partnership. When a bee visits a flower to collect nectar, tiny grains of pollen stick to its body. When the bee flies to the next flower, it transfers that pollen, which helps the flower make seeds. This process, called pollination, allows many plants to bloom and reproduce. Without bees, many of the fruits and vegetables we eat would not exist.

Lesson 29: A Comet Passes By

Lesson 30: The Rainforest Layers

diverse — made up of many different types or kinds

layer — one level in a stacked set of environments or materials

Example

A rainforest is not just one environment — it is four stacked on top of each other. The tallest trees form the canopy, where most of the sunlight lands. Rainforests are the most diverse ecosystems on Earth, meaning they contain more types of living things than anywhere else.
